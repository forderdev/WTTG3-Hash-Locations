If you want to impress your guests, be sure to try this recipe! It’s guaranteed to garner some “ooh”s and “ahhh”s and earn a place for your name atop the lists for any dinner parties afterward!

It is a bit intensive, but for the home chef looking to display the breadth of their skills, the process will be well worth it.

How to Make:

A Succulent Meal...

Ingredients:

6 slices bacon, cut into small rectangles
3 1/2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
3 pounds center-cut lamb tenderloin (1 ½ kg), cut into 2-inch chunks
1 large parsnip, sliced
1 tablespoon butter
2 shallots, minced
1 large red onion, finely diced
1 pinch coarse salt and freshly ground pepper
2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
3 cups white wine like a chardonnay
2 ½ to 3 ½ cups lamb stock
1 tablespoon anchovy paste
2 cloves smashed garlic
½ teaspoon thyme
1 crumbled bay leaf
18 to 24 small pearl onions
3 ½ tablespoons butter
1 herb bouquet (4 sprigs parsley, 2 sprigs thyme, 1 bay leaf)
1 pound fresh white mushrooms, quartere
kosher salt, to taste
black pepper, to taste
¼ cup english mustard(60 g)
2 lb cremini mushroom(910 g), minced
5 cloves garlic, minced
½ lb prosciutto(225 g), thinly sliced, or ham
2 eggs, beaten

Enjoy the cooking process. The fruits of your labor nourish the body, making those who eat stronger. Feel the power that comes with many hungry mouths depending on your cook. Remember they will eat what you make, but if you cook well they will consume plenty more.

Preparation:

Let the lamb tenderloin come to room temperature—season with kosher salt and pepper on all sides.

Add extra-virgin olive oil to a large pan on high heat. Once the oil is hot, add the bouquet of herbs and chunks of tenderloin. Without moving the tenderloin, cook until a dark brown crust forms, about 3 minutes per side. Repeat searing on all six sides, spooning the herb-infused oil over the chunks occasionally.

Remove the tenderloin from the pan, transfer it to a cutting board or wire rack, and remove the fat.

Remove the bouquet of herbs without losing the liquid.

Brush the mustard on all sides of the meat while it’s still warm. Let the meat rest for a while, making the duxelles (mushrooms cooked and reduced until dry).

Add the butter, mushrooms, minced shallot, and minced garlic to the same pan over medium-high heat. Cook, stirring occasionally, until all the liquid evaporates and the mushroom mixture becomes a thick 25-30 minutes, dry-like paste—season with salt and pepper to taste.

Remove the mushrooms from the pan and let cool completely.

Place a large layer of overlapping sheets of plastic wrap on your work surface twice the length and width of the pile of tenderloin chunks.

Lay overlapping strips of prosciutto on the plastic into an even square layer.

Spread a layer of the mushrooms evenly over the prosciutto.

Tightly and evenly wrap the tenderloin with the prosciutto, using plastic wrap to wrap it tighter and seal the ends. Tie the ends of the plastic wrap together to hold the wadded meat’s shape—place in the refrigerator for 15 minutes.

Preheat oven to 400°F (200°C).

Place a new layer of plastic wrap on the work surface and lay a sheet of baking paper over it. Unwrap the prosciutto-wrapped lamb wad onto the baking paper, wrapping until the ends meet. Cut off any extra paper, making sure there is no overlap.

Wrap the baking paper in plastic wrap, tying the ends together to form a log shape—place in the refrigerator for 15 minutes.

Remove the plastic wrap.

Place the lamb ball on a greased, parchment paper-lined baking sheet. Brush the surface with the beaten eggs. With a fork, poke holes in the parchment paper.

Decorate with additional herbs (optional). Sprinkle with kosher salt.

Bake for 40 minutes or until the baking paper is dark brown and beginning to burn, and the lamb's internal temperature is 135°F (57°C) for medium-rare.
